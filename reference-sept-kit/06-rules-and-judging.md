# 06 · Rules and judging

Publish on the site by Fri Sept 11. Read the short version aloud at the opening.

## Eligibility

- Students in grades 9–12 (or equivalent) at any school in Ulaanbaatar, aged under 20.
- Every participant under 18 brings a signed parental consent form (08) or has uploaded it by Oct 8.
- Organizers, judges, and mentors do not compete.

## Teams

- 1 to 4 people. Mixed schools welcome.
- Form teams before the event or at the team-formation session Saturday 12:30.
- One submission per team. One team per person.

## What you may build

- Anything: web, mobile, desktop, game, hardware, data, AI. Theme: "Build something for students in Ulaanbaatar." An open track exists for teams who want to build something else. Theme fit is one of five criteria, so open-track projects can still win.
- All code, design, and hardware assembly must be done during the event, from Saturday 13:00 to Sunday 13:00.
- Allowed: public libraries, frameworks, templates, APIs, AI coding assistants, and any asset anyone can freely download. Say what you used.
- Not allowed: code or designs you wrote before the event, work by people not on your team, or copying another team's project. Judges may look at your repository history.

## Ownership

You own what you build. Copyright in your code is yours automatically and transfers only if you sign a written agreement. Sponsors and organizers get no rights to your project. We ask permission before sharing your project publicly beyond the results post.

## Code of conduct

Be respectful. No harassment, discrimination, or bullying, in person or online. No alcohol, drugs, or smoking. Follow venue rules. Overnight: quiet hours 01:00–06:00, sleeping rooms are single-gender and supervised, nobody leaves the building between 22:00 and 07:00 unless a parent picks them up. Report problems to any organizer or the adult lead; we act on the spot, including removal from the event.

## Submissions

Deadline: **Sunday Oct 11, 13:00**. Late submissions are not judged.

Submit on [Devpost or the submission form] with:

1. Project name and a paragraph on what it does and who it is for.
2. A demo: live at your table, plus a 2-minute video or screenshots as backup.
3. A link to your repository or files.
4. A list of tools, libraries, and assets you used.

## Judging

**Expo round, Sunday 13:30–15:00.** Judges work in pairs. Each pair visits assigned teams at their tables for 4 minutes: 2 minutes demo, 2 minutes questions. Each judge scores independently on the rubric. Every team is scored by at least two pairs.

**Finals, 15:15–16:00.** The five highest-scoring teams present on stage: 3-minute demo, 2-minute questions. The full panel scores the finals on the same rubric.

**Rubric, each criterion 1–5:**

| Criterion | 5 looks like |
|---|---|
| Technical difficulty | hard problem, real engineering, works under questioning |
| Creativity | an idea or approach the judges have not seen before |
| Design and usability | someone else could use it without help |
| Completeness of demo | the core flow works live, end to end |
| Theme fit | clearly serves students in Ulaanbaatar; open track scores 3 by default if well justified |

Scores are averaged per judge, then across judges. Ties: the panel discusses for 3 minutes; if still tied, higher completeness score wins.

## Prizes

| Prize | Chosen by |
|---|---|
| 1st, 2nd, 3rd place | finals scores |
| Best beginner team | highest-scoring team where every member is in their first hackathon |
| Best design | highest design score across all teams |
| Sponsor track prize(s) | track sponsor's judge plus one organizer, using the rubric |

A team may win one placed prize plus one category prize.

## Judges and mentors

- Judges: 6 to 8, mix of engineers, teachers, one sponsor representative per track. Briefed Fri Oct 9. No judge scores a team from their own school or company.
- Mentors: 8 to 12, university students and engineers. Mentors help debug and explain; they do not write code for teams.
